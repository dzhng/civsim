## What landed

**TypeGPU candidate scene** (`candidates/typegpu/battleScene.ts`)
- `replaceCrowdAssets(published, validate?)`: stages a whole new audience behind one `beginGpuAdmission` scope, validates, carries `crowd.admitted()` through the last prepared camera (`battleSceneCamera(lastCamera, …, lastTime)`), submits the staged precompute through the frame's own encoder, re-validates after admission, and only then disposes the installed generation and bumps the epoch. Failure or disposal at any stage disposes only the stage; the scene's `lifecycle.run` still owns the post-operation `check()`, so a scene closed mid-stage releases the new crowd rather than installing it.
- `admittedSeatingIdentity()` / `verifyAdmittedSeating()` / `admittedCrowdPoses()` / `debugSoldierAnim()` — all answered off the already-shared `createCrowdAudienceDiagnostics` the candidate audience spreads; no new scanning machinery.
- `stats().depth` = `frame.depthStats()` (the real attachment, not a shape).
- `terrainScene.ts` gained `committedGeneration()`. **Ownership note:** the brief named `terrain.ts`, but the committed generation belongs to the scene-level terrain owner (`terrainScene.ts`, matching raw's `world/terrainScene.ts`); `terrain.ts` is a single mesh pipeline and has no generation to commit.

**Facade** (`src/live/NativeBattleRenderer.ts`) — `rawScene` split into two predicates named for real capability: `crowdWorld` (the crowd generation both selected worlds own — seating identity, verification, reload, per-soldier reads, depth/shadow/crowd/grass stats) and `rawWorld` (the debug-block upload only the raw world implements, which also gates its terrain/overlay/environment content and its published `substrate`/`projection`). A world that owns the crowd but not the content reports those as null **and** names them in `openObligations` rather than publishing an empty scene.

## Ownership choices worth auditing

- **Staged-replacement sequence is duplicated with the raw scene, deliberately.** The two differ in exclusion primitive (`busy` vs `createSceneLifecycle`), encoder ownership (device vs `frame.nativeEncoder`) and upload sync-ness, so a shared helper would need ~5 injected callbacks to save ~18 lines, and removing the duplication would require restructuring the raw world (out of scope, deleted at M9). The part that genuinely must not drift — the admitted-state reads — is already one owner. Drift is pinned instead by parallel test names in `web/tests/nativeSceneLifecycle.test.ts` and `typegpuSceneLifecycle.test.ts`.
- **TypeGPU reports `substrate: null`** rather than borrowing `raw-webgpu` or inventing an identity constant; it owes the obligation instead.
- **Grass stats ride with `terrain`** and are therefore unavailable for the converted world. Kept the existing grouping rather than publishing a half-shaped `terrain`.
