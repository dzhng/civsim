**Remaining or new problems**

- **Against the reference:** A is closer in relief and large-form readability. Both lack its distinct rocky peaks, varied slope shapes and convincing transitions between exposed rock and vegetated pockets.
- **A:** Broad, stretched rock faces; sharp, occasionally sawtoothed grass boundaries; scattered boulders and trees often appear placed on top rather than integrated.
- **B:** Excessive, similarly scaled ridges and channels; grassy coverage weakens rock-face definition. Denser trees obscure terrain and buildings, especially in the close crop.
- **Real gain in B:** Some smaller branching terrain features and gentler local transitions. These gains are outweighed regionally by lost relief and repetitive surface structure.
