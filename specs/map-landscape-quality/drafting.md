# Draft synthesis and remaining uncertainty

Three independent drafts inspected the repository from the same brief: a fewest-slices draft, a risk-first draft, and a seam-quality draft run with Claude Opus. The canonical plan combines them; none was adopted wholesale.

| Draft | Useful contribution | Decision |

| Risk first | Prove depth/picking integration and bounded runtime before expensive art; preserve physical terrain | Bring composition and residency to 02–03; avoid bundling those risks into one probe. |

## Alternatives resolved

A shared WGSL kernel hosted by two renderers would preserve duplicate composition, resource and lighting ownership. Choose the existing physical substrate for both maps. Keep strategic input interpretation distinct from GPU material response; sharing does not require one universal generator.

A whole-world fine mesh or texture is not the default. Start with bounded cached regular tiles and coarse overview; consider clipmaps only if measured traversal evidence justifies them. Tile geometry and queries describe the same presented revision. Battle's physical query retains its separate meaning.

An erosion simulator or new D8 river network would introduce new geographic and gameplay questions. Preserve source waterways and improve their boundaries and response. No new locale schema is needed when current site features already choose battle character. Settlement seating stays local instead of flattening holes through entire ranges.

The spike's rounded crowns reduced submitted triangles substantially, but did not establish final quality or frame-time performance. Refine the shared model family and projected detail representation rather than freeze those crowns. Preserve species mixing and reservations that the spike simplified.

Do not inherit another spec's approval rules, obsolete flat-sea assumptions, or an unverified claim that a battle baseline is already red. Establish baseline failures in 01, record evidence, and distinguish existing defects from regressions.

## Recursive fog audit

The broad visual pass was split into landform (04), material (05), crowns (06), distribution (07), water geography (08), water response (09), and light (10). Each freezes the others and names a focused artifact. Coast placement and water appearance are different inputs and verdicts. Production composition was split into an early minimal depth/picking proof (02), geographic layers (11), entities/labels (12), and final ownership cutover (14).

The remaining algorithm freedoms are deliberately delegated in the owning slice: ridge extraction/profile, tile resolution within measured bounds, crown construction, density curves, and restrained material constants. Failed evidence returns to that owner. If a pass needs an additional independent variable, split the slice and update the handoff before broadening the implementation.

## Refactor-clean and conversation audit

Architecture names one owner for source interpretation, surface geometry/query, material response, scenery identity, environment, tile scheduling and campaign composition. Temporary coexistence is limited to the existing production renderer while the new production owner is proven through a thin lab route; 14 removes remaining displaced passes. Shared pieces are extracted when actual consumers exist, not as speculative wrappers.

The user's decisive constraints are retained in the README: new worktree, reference quality, battle character rather than exact geography, shared logic where simpler, and autonomous spikes without permission pauses. The previous exploration is historical and points to the canonical plan. No unresolved product choice blocks slice 01. Open technical uncertainty has a bounded experiment and acceptance surface in the ladder.
