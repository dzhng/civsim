## Shared limitations and verdict

- **Terrain coverage:** Neither side visibly loses a broad grass region or the central platform. Both use stark rectangular material patches and straight yellowish strips at their joins; these read as fixture transitions rather than a natural continuous ground surface.
- **Wall/apron:** Large flat color regions and hard lighting boundaries limit the sense of material and depth on both sides. The serrated apron boundary in the low-angle views is a shared visible defect. The initial wall-band discrepancy is separate and must not be dismissed because the repeat pair agrees.
- **Water:** Both show an abrupt sheet-like termination, angular teal cutouts/fragments beside the platform, and a blunt distant water edge against the sky in the horizon views. Ripple texture appears continuous within the visible water surface; shoreline integration is visibly weak on both sides. Still images cannot establish temporal stability or the cause of these artifacts.

**Verdict:** No defensible overall winner. The initial pair has a clear lighting-boundary difference whose correct shape cannot be determined from these images alone. The other pairs are visually close, with prominent shared fixture limitations; that similarity does not establish that either rendering is correct.
