# Contact-state detail opportunity

The ordinary battle fixture reaches the canonical benchmark tick and exact state
hash using the scenario owner's opening orders. All seven saved samples retain
that hash, paused-but-unfrozen rendering and advancing non-skipped frame IDs.
Post-run checks also match each renderer-consumed camera's center, distance, yaw
and pitch to its requested pose. Preparation is not a performance measurement.

These are seven selected camera poses at the fixed contact state, not a replay of
the whole tour or later combat. They omit the tour's closest views. The raw count
key is appearance / projected-size band / fresh policy classification. That last
field is recomputed without history, so it is **not actual admitted LOD**; the
actual renderer histogram is retained separately. Size bands use the planner's
canonical standing-height metric, not the rasterized body bounding box.

Most main-view soldiers in the selected tactical and horizon poses fall between
18 and 32 physical projected pixels. Phalanx contributes roughly 850–1,230 soldiers
in that band; sword and light-spear classes contribute more. This makes a bounded
phalanx geometry control useful before extending bake/calibration work. Wide
900-distance views put all visible soldiers below 18 pixels and exercise a
different representation mix. No whole-tour cost distribution is claimed.

Next compare original and candidate phalanx geometry at this identical contact
state, with normal grass/post/shadows and unchanged camera. A diagnostic near-mesh
replacement may measure the potential savings; it is not a proposal to replace
near detail at every zoom. Adoption still needs a calibrated interval, temporal
quality, independently appropriate shadow detail and measured net savings.
