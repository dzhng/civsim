# Rounded joint grasp — rejected

A compact grasp is useful, but smoothing a shared finger template still produces
stacked loops rather than a natural fist. This no-Boolean study removed the
prior machined seat and reduced the exposed palm length. It retained a broad
featureless back, a straight transverse finger-root edge, repeated U-shaped
digits and a hooked thumb with a blunt projecting end. Neither it nor the
[settled closure](../power-grip-closure/review.md) meets the natural-hand target.

The fresh unprimed reviewer inspected six A/B sheets, all 24 two-times crops,
A-only whole-body context and the local Rome II supplemental reference. Main
inspection agreed with the rejection. Shield views 0 and 3 fully hide the hand;
there is no new whole-body or timing evidence. Equipment placement changes
alongside anatomy, so pixel differences cannot isolate either contribution.

## Why this construction did not solve the form

The palm is still one oval loft with a single dorsal dome. The first three
digits share the same joint arrangement with only small offsets. Circular
cross-sections and interpolated centerlines softened the bars without changing
that repetition. A subsequent pass needs individually authored metacarpal slopes
and phalange directions, a compressed opposing thumb pad and a continuous wrist
transition—not another width or smoothing adjustment to this template.

The wrist bridge now minimizes squared cross-boundary edge lengths through a
monotone triangulation. This reduced its measured maximum local dihedral from
139 degrees to 60.65 degrees and removed the CPU-visible crease without moving
the retained boundary. It does not establish an anatomical wrist or independently
reviewed reusable source improvement; this entire candidate remains rejected.

## Physical gates

The local donor edit preserved all 10,822 required non-hand positions and weights,
plus rig names, parents and rest matrices. The new body has 24,505 vertices;
maximum weight-sum error was 1.64e-7. Sampled nonadjacent hand self-intersections
were zero. Strict human and isolated-heavy bakes passed.

Connectivity failed: the body has components of 24,497 and eight vertices. The
small remesh fragment is approximately 0.39 by 0.29 by 0.47 mm at
(-0.5908, -0.0686, 0.9240) m. Manifold edges alone did not detect it. No fragment
was silently removed or validation relaxed.

All 930 expanded poses were clear for the grips, guard, pommel and shield
supports. The blade was clear in ready, walk, run, pronation and combined
bend/pronation. Bend alone still failed in 71 samples, with at most 138
triangle pairs, versus 112 samples and at most 55 pairs for the frozen original
blade. Fewer affected samples do not compensate for the larger overlap count;
this is not an all-clear fit. Coverage was ready 1, walk 109, run 97 and 241
samples per clinical clip.

Frame-aware nearest sampled finger support gaps were 2.01, 3.26, 1.37 and
1.31 mm. The first two finger regions had no samples within 1.5 mm. These are
nearest surface samples, not measured contact area; zero intersection is not
proof of secure contact. No minimum wall-thickness claim from the prior Boolean
study carries over to this candidate.

The full sword assembly retained its pairwise dimensions within 1.01e-7 m;
sword parts and shield grip retained topology, UVs and weights. Shield body and
boss positions/topology were exact. The right grasp axis gained a volar component
and all sword parts moved by one rigid rotation; shield supports remained fitted
to the actual shield. No other gear was promoted.
