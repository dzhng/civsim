# Independent image review

Reviewed all eight full frames and six paired native-pixel crops. Coordinates below refer to the original 2880×1800 frames; crops show A on the left, B on the right. No implementation or intended winner was consulted.

## One-sided differences

- **Tactical initial: A has visibly weaker/missing formation shadows.** At x1260–1610, y1240–1425, the center formation's feet end over almost unshaded ground in A. B has clear soft elongated shadows extending downward from its rows. This is visible at whole-frame scale too, especially under the center and right foreground formations. B is better grounded. See `initial-shadow-A-left-B-right.png` (2× nearest-neighbor enlargement).
- **Tactical initial: rear-center soldiers are substantially darker in A.** At x1335–1550, y430–605, B has many more bright body/gear highlights, while A has dark vertical lanes. Formation outline, density and label remain consistent. This is a lighting difference rather than evidence of missing soldiers. See `initial-rear-light-A-left-B-right.png` (2×).
- **Tactical settled: those conspicuous initial differences are no longer apparent.** The matching center-foot crop shows soft row shadows in both, and full frames show the bright rear-center formation in both. See `settled-shadow-A-left-B-right.png`. There are fine pixel differences, but I cannot identify a missing formation, displaced label, changed silhouette or materially altered shadow footprint from this pair.
- **Wide settled and horizon settled:** no concrete one-sided content loss or distortion identified. Both retain the same formation arrangement, terrain outline, label arrangement and central cue. This is a visual observation, not a claim of pixel identity.

## Shared shortcomings and framing limits

- **Horizon labels collide heavily.** x1240–1640, y830–902: all four rows of large “Holding” plates stack into an almost continuous wall, horizontally overwrite letters, and obscure rear formations and much of the central cue. `horizon-labels-A-left-B-right.png` (crop x1220–1650, y815–955, 2×) makes this unambiguous in both images. This framing can test distant terrain silhouette and sky, but cannot support a detailed soldier/grounding judgment: almost all the frame is sky and the soldiers occupy only a small central patch.
- **Hard artificial terrain boundaries in both.** Wide frames expose the entire yellow rectangular inner terrain inside a green rectangular outer plane; the front inner edge has a conspicuous bright rippled band at approximately y1438–1458. `wide-boundary-A-left-B-right.png` covers x1000–1600, y1400–1485. The outer plane itself ends abruptly against sky. These are shared visual characteristics; whether a test-map boundary is intentional cannot be determined from images.
- **Tactical framing clips the closest row.** At approximately y1640 onward, five foreground formations enter and continue beyond the bottom edge; their complete footprints and labels cannot be reviewed. The middle three rows are well separated and large enough to compare formation silhouettes and grounding. No one-sided clipping difference is visible.
- **Central cue survives in both settled tactical images.** x1240–1700, y785–1055: cream arc, orange wedge, thin guide lines and the “Holding” plate are present and aligned similarly. Ground speckling is conspicuous through/over the cue in both, and the wedge overlaps the formation. See `settled-cue-A-left-B-right.png`. Without intended interaction semantics, overlap alone is not evidence of a defect.

Static images cannot establish whether the initial shadow/highlight discrepancy causes a noticeable pop, how long it lasts, whether motion flickers, or whether the pixel speckling is stable. Settled equivalence does not erase the concrete initial grounding discrepancy.
