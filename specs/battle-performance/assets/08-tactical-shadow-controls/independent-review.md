## What I see

**A vs B — indistinguishable, no ground contact**
At full frame and in both 2x crops, A and B look identical to me; I could not find a single differing feature (formations, banners, grass stipple, UI all match). In both:
- Front rank: figures terminate at the feet with no darkening at all. They read as pasted onto the grass — no contact, no directional cue, nothing that tells you where the sun is. (high confidence)
- Middle rank block: same — the block's underside is the same green as open field; the formation has no seating on the ground. (high confidence)
- No acne, no doubled/detached shadows — because there is nothing to detach. No broad terrain darkening either. (high confidence)

**C — the only variant with readable contact**
- Full frame: soft dark pooling is visible under the left dark-armour block and along the front spear line without zooming. At that scale it reads mostly as a *shaded patch the formation sits in* rather than per-soldier shadows, but it is legible ground contact and it is directional (mass sits low-left of the figures). (high confidence it's visible; medium confidence on the direction, since the blur makes the offset hard to call)
- Front crop: each soldier has a soft elongated shadow smearing toward the lower-left, merging with neighbours into a continuous smudge between the rows. Per-man separation is largely lost — you get a rank-wide band, not 30 attached shadows. (high confidence)
- Suspicious detachment: in the mid-row of C-front several dark blobs sit between rows rather than touching the feet that should own them. This may be genuine peter-panning or just occlusion by the row in front — I can't resolve it at 2x. (low-medium confidence)
- One-sided loss: C also darkens ground well beyond the silhouettes — a broad low-contrast wash under and around each block, including the wide band across the middle of C-front. That's a real cost: the field loses some of its even, bright read, and shadow contrast is spent on terrain rather than on the figures. (medium-high confidence)
- No acne or visible shadow-map stair-stepping at these scales. (medium confidence — 2x is not enough to rule acne out)

## Verdict

**C is less wrong.** It's the only variant where ground contact is readable at full-frame scale; A and B are a **tie** and both fail the basic test — soldiers do not touch the ground in either. C's flaws (merged/blurry shadows, broad terrain darkening, possible detachment in the mid-row) are real and worth fixing, but they're refinement problems on top of a working effect, versus no effect at all.

## Limits of these stills

- Single sun angle, single camera, single pause frame. Contact quality can differ badly at other sun elevations or closer zooms, and none of that is visible here.
- A and B being pixel-identical to my eye is a judgement by inspection; I was denied shell access to crop/diff further, so I can't prove they're the same image versus two settings that happen to look the same from this camera.
- 2x is too coarse to confirm or rule out acne, peter-panning, or cascade seams — the C mid-row blobs specifically need a tighter crop.
- Nothing here speaks to performance, temporal stability, or shadow swim under motion, and I'm not inferring any.
- I'm treating none of the three as the reference; C being the most legible doesn't mean its darkening amount or shadow softness is correct.
